River No. 2 Beach

A coastal escape where a river meets the Atlantic, framed by sand and forest on the Freetown Peninsula.

Where freshwater estuaries meet wild Atlantic surf

River No. 2 Beach brings together a pristine sandy shoreline, a calm meandering river mouth, and an emerald forested backdrop. Celebrated as one of West Africa’s most idyllic stretches of coast, it offers a tranquil haven for swimming in clear estuary waters, walking along empty beaches, and experiencing genuine coastal hospitality managed directly by the local community association. Plan ahead to confirm seasonal conditions and access available community-guided river trips.

Highlights and things to do

Use these ideas to shape your visit; check local tides and conditions before taking part.

surfing

Enjoy the coast

Take a shoreline walk along kilometers of powdery quartz sand and soak in the Atlantic atmosphere. Follow local safety advice regarding ocean currents and designated swimming sandbanks.

Relaxed exploration north_east
kayaking

See the river mouth

Observe the majestic confluence where fresh mountain runoff mingles with turquoise ocean water. Hire a traditional wooden pirogue or paddle upriver into lush mangrove tributaries.

Riverboat excursion north_east
restaurant

Local hospitality

Dine on freshly caught barracuda, lobster, and sweet cassava prepared over open mangrove embers at the community-operated open-air beach pavilion.

Field Advice

Essential visitor information

directions_car

Getting There

Accessible via the paved Peninsula Circular Road from Lumley. 45–60 minute scenic drive from Freetown; 4WD recommended during rainy months.

wb_sunny

Best Season

November through May brings calm water, dry sunshine, and crystal visibility. June to October brings dramatic surf and lush green hills.

villa

Overnight Stays

Simple beachfront guest chalets and screened tent platforms managed by the community association. Advance notice recommended for weekends.

eco

Community Fee

A nominal facility entry fee supports village sanitation, school bursaries, and coastal conservation upkeep.
