One of Africa's densest primate sanctuaries

Tiwai Island shelters over 135 bird species, rare pygmy hippopotamuses, and 11 distinct species of non-human primates including the Diana monkey, Western chimpanzee, King colobus, and Red colobus.

An ancient riverine refuge for West Africa’s rarest wildlife

Located in the Southern Province near the Gola Rainforest National Park, Tiwai (meaning “Big Island” in the Mende language) is an inland island nestled along the winding Moa River. Formed thousands of years ago, its isolated ecosystem became an ecological haven where tropical primary rainforest has flourished undisturbed.

Tiwai is world-renowned as a pioneering model for community-based ecotourism and conservation. Co-managed by the Environmental Foundation for Africa (EFA), Njala University, and eight neighboring host communities, all tourism proceeds directly fund local village schools, clean water projects, and ranger patrols.

Immerse yourself in wildlife tracking, boat cruises, and nocturnal forest expeditions led by certified Mende guides.

Essential visitor information

directions_car

Getting There

Drive via Bo and Kenema to Potoru, then continue to Kambama village. A short 10-minute motorboat trip across the Moa River delivers visitors to the visitor center.

calendar_month

Best Season

October to April (dry season) is ideal for easy walking on trails, calm river boating, and clear wildlife viewing. The wet season brings intense greenery and bird activity.

camping

Tented Eco-Camp

Tiwai offers raised, covered wooden platforms with tents, clean mattresses, mosquito nets, solar power, and freshly prepared local home cooking by community hosts.

hiking

What to Pack

Pack sturdy waterproof hiking shoes, lightweight long-sleeve clothing, binoculars, torch/headlamp for night safaris, insect repellent, and cash for community fees.

Plan your wildlife safari to Tiwai Island

We coordinate private 4WD expeditions from Freetown or Bo, community guide bookings, boat crossings, and eco-camp reservations.
