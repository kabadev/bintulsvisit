Sierra Leone lies on the Atlantic coast of West Africa. Its capital, Freetown, is a vibrant gateway to coastal fishing villages, historical island journeys, and verdant highlands inland.

Natural attractions

From the Freetown Peninsula’s white-sand shores to the Upper Guinean rainforest belt, mangrove estuaries, and remote river sanctuaries, visitors encounter one of the planet’s richest conservation frontiers.

Heritage and history

Understand the places you visit through the stories behind them, honoring ancestral ties and living expressions.

Stories of the past

Historic locations across the archipelago and peninsula—including Bunce Island's preserved fortress ruins, the historic Cotton Tree grounds, and Freetown’s architectural heritage—offer vital spaces to reflect on the transatlantic slave trade, the return of freed peoples, and Sierra Leone's enduring resilience.

Living traditions

Local languages including Krio, Mende, and Temne, vibrant textiles like Gara cloth, rhythmic Bubu music, and hearty coastal gastronomy such as cassava leaf stew and fresh Atlantic seafood define daily life. Traveling here means listening attentively and seeking local perspectives with warmth and mutual honor.

photo_camera
Community Etiquette Always seek local perspectives and ask courteous permission before photographing community elders or ceremonies.
